There is a persistent belief that going direct to your bank should get you the best mortgage rate because you are "cutting out the middleman." In practice, the opposite is usually true. Here is how the UK mortgage distribution market actually works, and why a broker frequently beats the rate your bank manager will quote you.

## The intermediary-distributed product market

Roughly 87% of new UK mortgage business by value is now placed through intermediaries — brokers and mortgage advisers. That share has grown steadily for two decades. As a result, lenders increasingly design products specifically for the intermediary channel.

These "broker-only" or "intermediary-exclusive" products are not available if you walk into a branch or apply on the lender's consumer website. The same lender will quote you one rate at the counter and a different, lower rate through a broker for an otherwise identical product. The reason is operational: intermediaries arrive with a fully packaged case (income, deposit, ID, property details, affordability already checked) so the lender's underwriting cost is lower. They share that saving with the broker channel.

A typical example today: a high-street bank's direct 5-year fix at 75% LTV might be advertised at 4.32%. The same bank's intermediary 5-year fix at 75% LTV is 4.09%. On a £250,000 mortgage over 25 years, that gap is roughly £33 a month, or £2,000 across the fixed term.

## The procuration fee model

Brokers are paid by lenders, not by you, on the vast majority of cases. The procuration fee — "proc fee" — is typically 0.35%-0.45% of the loan size for residential mortgages and slightly higher for buy-to-let. On a £250,000 mortgage that is roughly £900-£1,125 paid by the lender to the broker on completion.

Two implications:

1. There is usually no fee from the broker to you for a standard residential case.
2. The fee is set by lenders at broadly similar levels, so brokers are not financially incentivised to push you toward one lender over another for commission reasons. (This is an FCA-regulated point — brokers must recommend the most suitable product, not the highest-paying one.)

For complex cases — adverse credit, complicated income structures, very high loan amounts, expat applications — brokers often charge an additional fee, typically £300-£800, because the work involved is genuinely greater. A reputable broker discloses this upfront before you commit.

## Lenders you cannot reach direct

Several categories of lender simply do not deal with the public:

- **Specialist building societies** — Cambridge Building Society, Mansfield, Hanley Economic and a dozen others. Often the right answer for non-standard cases (older borrowers, contractors, properties with unusual construction).
- **Private banks** — Coutts, Arbuthnot Latham, Investec. Relevant for high-net-worth borrowers, often offering bespoke underwriting and competitive rates for £1m+ loans.
- **Specialist BTL lenders** — Paragon, Landbay, Fleet Mortgages. The market leaders for portfolio landlords and limited-company SPVs.
- **Adverse credit specialists** — Pepper Money, Vida Homeloans, Kensington. The right answer when high-street lenders decline you for past credit issues.

If your case is anything other than vanilla — and many are not — these lenders are the ones who will actually approve you, and you cannot reach them without an intermediary.

## How broker stress-tests compare

When you apply direct to a lender, you get that lender's affordability assessment. If they say no, you start again somewhere else and your credit file picks up another search.

A broker runs your numbers through multiple lenders' affordability calculators before you formally apply. The variance is genuinely surprising:

- Lender A might offer you 4.2x income
- Lender B might offer you 4.8x income
- Lender C might consider 5.5x income because you earn over £75,000

Same applicant, same evidence, three very different borrowing ceilings. The broker's job is to identify the lender whose stress test treats your specific income profile most favourably — bonuses, commission, second jobs, dividends, retained company profits — without you having to apply blindly.

## When going direct DOES make sense

There are real cases where the broker channel does not add value:

1. **Existing customer retention deals.** If you are remortgaging and your existing lender's product transfer rate matches whole-of-market rates, the convenience of a same-week PT with no paperwork wins. Always get a broker quote alongside it as a benchmark.
2. **Very simple cases at very low LTVs.** A 40% LTV remortgage with a clean employed PAYE income on a standard house is genuinely commoditised. The rate gap between direct and intermediary tends to be smallest at the lowest LTVs.
3. **You have a private banking relationship and your private banker is offering preferential pricing.** This is a real category, though only relevant to perhaps 1-2% of borrowers.

## What to expect from a good broker

A competent broker will:

- Take a free initial conversation to understand your situation and timing
- Provide a range of illustrations from at least 3-4 lenders before you commit
- Explain the rate, fees, ERCs and overpayment terms in plain English
- Handle the paperwork with the lender from application through to offer
- Stay in touch through completion and diary you for review before your deal ends

## Practical next steps

- Get a broker quote alongside any direct quote you receive — there is no cost to comparing.
- Ask the broker explicitly whether they are whole-of-market or restricted to a panel.
- Check that the broker is FCA-authorised by searching the FCA Register.
