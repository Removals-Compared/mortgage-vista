Remortgaging is the routine financial admin most homeowners get wrong by doing it too late. Done well, it can save several hundred pounds a month. Done badly, you slip onto a Standard Variable Rate (SVR) at 7%-plus and lose a year's worth of savings before you notice. Here is how the process actually runs.

## When to start: six months before your deal ends

Most lenders allow you to lock in a new rate up to six months before your current fix expires. That window matters for two reasons:

1. If rates fall between locking and completion, most lenders let you switch to the cheaper product without penalty.
2. If rates rise, you keep the rate you locked. There is no symmetric risk.

Six months out is the right time to start gathering documents and asking your broker for an illustration. Three months out is the right time to formally apply.

If your deal has already ended and you are sitting on the SVR, the answer is "this week, not next week." On a £200,000 balance, the gap between a 7% SVR and a 4.2% remortgage is around £325 a month. Every month of delay is real money.

## Product transfer vs full remortgage

You have two routes:

**Product transfer (PT):** Stay with your existing lender, switch to one of their new deals. Minimal paperwork — usually no new affordability assessment, no valuation, no legal work. Completion is typically same-day or within a fortnight.

**Full remortgage:** Apply to a new lender, who underwrites you fresh and handles all the legal work to redeem the old mortgage and register the new one. Takes 4-8 weeks but often offers materially better rates.

The decision rule is straightforward: get an illustration for both. If the new lender beats your existing lender's PT rate by enough to justify the hassle (typically 0.2%+ on a free-legal product), remortgage. Otherwise, take the PT.

A common mistake is assuming loyalty pays. It does not. PT rates are usually competitive but not the best on the market — lenders price them to retain you cheaply, not to win business.

## Document checklist

For a full remortgage, expect to provide:

- Last 3 months' payslips (or 2 years' SA302s and tax year overviews if self-employed)
- Last 3 months' bank statements for every current account
- P60 from the last tax year
- Photo ID (passport or driving licence)
- Proof of address dated within the last 3 months
- Latest mortgage statement showing the outstanding balance
- Buildings insurance schedule

A PT typically requires none of this — the lender already has it.

## The lender's underwriting process

A typical timeline once you submit a full application:

- **Day 1-3:** Soft assessment, AIP confirmed
- **Day 3-7:** Documents reviewed by an underwriter
- **Day 7-14:** Valuation instructed and completed
- **Day 14-21:** Mortgage offer issued
- **Day 21-42:** Legal work, redemption statement requested from old lender, completion

Underwriters are looking for three things: that the property is suitable security, that you can afford the payments under the lender's stress test, and that nothing in your file contradicts what you have declared. The cleaner the paperwork, the faster the decision.

## Valuation types

Lenders use one of three approaches:

1. **Automated Valuation Model (AVM):** A computer-driven valuation using comparable sales data. Free, instant, and used for low-LTV remortgages on standard properties.
2. **Desktop valuation:** A surveyor reviews the property on file and online without visiting. Used at slightly higher LTVs or for properties the AVM cannot price confidently.
3. **Physical valuation:** A surveyor visits the property. Required for high-LTV, non-standard construction, or anything unusual.

For a typical 60-75% LTV remortgage on a standard semi or terrace, expect an AVM. If the lender insists on a physical, it usually adds 1-2 weeks.

## Legal work: free-legal vs cashback

Most remortgage products come in two flavours:

- **Free legal:** The lender appoints a panel solicitor who handles a basic remortgage at no cost to you. Cheap, but slower and less responsive — you are one of hundreds of files on a desk.
- **Cashback:** The lender pays you £250-£500 on completion and you appoint your own solicitor (typically £400-£700 for a remortgage). Faster, more responsive, marginally more expensive net.

If your case is straightforward, free-legal is fine. If you have multiple charges, a flying freehold, leasehold complications, or a tight timetable, pay for your own solicitor.

## The ERC trap

Early Repayment Charges (ERCs) catch borrowers who try to remortgage before their fixed period ends. On a typical 5-year fix the ERC is 1%-5% of the outstanding balance, falling each year.

Two scenarios where the trap bites:

- **Mid-term move to a much cheaper rate.** Tempting when rates fall sharply, but the ERC almost always exceeds the rate saving across the remaining term.
- **Releasing equity for a deposit on a second property.** Often better achieved through a further advance from your existing lender, which is treated as a separate loan and avoids the ERC.

Always ask your broker to model the all-in cost including ERC before you commit to breaking a fix early.

## Practical next steps

- Diary your deal end date and set a reminder for six months out.
- Check your credit file before you apply — fix any errors that take time to clear.
- Ask for both a PT illustration and a whole-of-market remortgage quote before deciding.
