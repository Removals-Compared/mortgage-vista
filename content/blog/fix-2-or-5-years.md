The 2-year versus 5-year fix question is the single most common one I am asked, and the answer has shifted as Bank Rate has fallen to 3.75%. The maths is no longer obvious in either direction, so the right choice depends on your circumstances rather than a one-size-fits-all rule.

## The current pricing picture

Across the main lenders at 75% LTV today:

- **2-year fix average:** around 4.39%
- **5-year fix average:** around 4.12%
- **10-year fix average:** around 4.45%

That 27 basis-point gap in favour of the 5-year deal is unusual. Historically the longer fix has carried a premium because lenders price in more interest-rate uncertainty over a longer horizon. The reason we see the inverse today is straightforward: swap markets expect Bank Rate to be lower in three to five years than it is now, so lenders can fund five-year lending more cheaply than two-year lending.

## What "inverted curve" actually means for you

If you take the 5-year fix at 4.12% on a £250,000 repayment mortgage over 25 years, your monthly payment is about £1,335. The 2-year fix at 4.39% costs roughly £1,375 — about £40 a month more, or £960 across the two-year term.

The 2-year fix only wins overall if, when you come to remortgage in two years' time, you can secure a new rate that is meaningfully lower than 4.12%. The break-even is roughly 3.55% on the three-year follow-on rate. Possible? Yes. Guaranteed? No.

## Early Repayment Charges (ERCs)

This is where the 5-year fix bites if your life changes. Typical ERC structure:

- Year 1: 5% of the outstanding balance
- Year 2: 4%
- Year 3: 3%
- Year 4: 2%
- Year 5: 1%

On a £250,000 balance, redeeming in year 3 of a 5-year fix costs £7,500. That is a real number that can wipe out the rate saving entirely if you sell, move abroad, or want to consolidate debt mid-term.

A few mitigations worth knowing:

1. **Overpayment allowance.** Most fixed deals allow 10% of the original balance to be overpaid each year without penalty.
2. **Porting.** Most lenders allow you to "port" the existing rate to a new property if you move. Porting is not guaranteed — the lender re-underwrites the new application — but for straightforward moves it usually works.
3. **Joint mortgages and divorce.** If you separate and one party buys the other out, ERCs typically still apply. Worth factoring in if a relationship is fragile.

## A decision framework

Use these three questions in order:

**1. What is your time horizon in the property?**

- Less than 3 years: 2-year fix (or even a tracker) almost always wins on flexibility
- 3-7 years: 5-year fix usually wins on cost and certainty
- Over 7 years: 5-year fix, then reassess

**2. How sensitive is your budget?**

If a £100/month payment swing would seriously stress your household budget, lean toward the longer fix. The peace of mind is worth more than the optionality.

**3. What is your view on rates?**

If you genuinely believe Bank Rate will be at 2.5% by mid-2027, the 2-year fix gives you the chance to capture that. If you think rates plateau around 3% or drift back up, the 5-year is the better hedge.

## Common scenarios

- **Young couple, first home, planning to stay 5+ years:** 5-year fix. The certainty supports family planning and the rate is genuinely competitive.
- **Buy-to-let landlord building a portfolio:** Often 2-year fix on each property, allowing portfolio refinancing as values rise.
- **Older borrower with strong income, low LTV (under 50%):** Either works. The rate gap is smaller at lower LTV bands, so flexibility usually wins.
- **Self-employed borrower with growing income:** 2-year fix often makes sense — affordability assessments based on stronger future accounts may unlock better products in two years.

## Practical next steps

- Get illustrations for both terms from your broker and compare total cost over the longer term, including any product fees.
- Ask explicitly about portability and the lender's track record on porting approvals.
- If you go for the 5-year fix, set up the 10% annual overpayment as a standing order from day one — the long-term interest saving is significant.
