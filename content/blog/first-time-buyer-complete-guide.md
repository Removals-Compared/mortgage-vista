Buying your first home is the single largest financial transaction most people will ever make, and the process has more moving parts than the brochures admit. This guide walks through it in the order you'll actually encounter each step, with the figures and timelines you can plan around.

## Stage 1: Building the deposit

Most first-time buyer mortgages today require a 5%-10% deposit, though 95% LTV products carry noticeably higher rates than 90% LTV ones. On a £250,000 purchase, a 10% deposit is £25,000. That's a stretch, but the rate saving over a five-year fix usually justifies the extra year of saving.

Three savings vehicles are worth knowing:

- **Lifetime ISA (LISA).** Save up to £4,000 per tax year and the government adds 25%, so £1,000 free if you maximise the allowance. The bonus is paid monthly. Withdraw for a first home up to £450,000 with no penalty. Open one before age 40, contribute until 50.
- **Help to Buy ISA (legacy).** Closed to new applicants in 2019, but if you opened one you can keep contributing until November 2029 and claim the bonus until December 2030.
- **Regular saver accounts.** The best high-street regular savers still pay 6%-7% on monthly deposits up to £200-£300. Useful for the portion of your deposit above the LISA cap.

## Stage 2: Getting your credit in shape

Six months before you apply, do three things:

1. Pull your statutory credit reports from Experian, Equifax and TransUnion. They differ. Lenders use all three.
2. Make sure you are on the electoral roll at your current address.
3. Stop applying for new credit. Each hard search knocks a handful of points off your score, and lenders dislike clusters of recent applications.

Close any unused store cards or credit lines you don't need. Lenders look at available credit, not just used credit.

## Stage 3: Agreement in Principle

An Agreement in Principle (AIP), sometimes called a Decision in Principle, is a soft-search statement from a lender saying they would, in principle, lend you a stated amount. Estate agents won't take your offer seriously without one. Most are valid for 60-90 days.

A broker can usually arrange an AIP within 24-48 hours from the documents below. Coming to a broker rather than applying direct also means the AIP is placed with a lender that fits your circumstances, not just the first one you walked into.

Document checklist:

- Last 3 months' payslips (or 2 years' SA302s if self-employed)
- Last 3 months' bank statements for every current account
- Photo ID and proof of address
- Deposit evidence: savings statements, gift letter if applicable
- P60 from the last tax year

## Stage 4: Finding lenders that say yes to first-timers

Not every lender is equally enthusiastic about first-time buyers. Specialist building societies like Skipton, Leeds, Coventry and Newcastle often offer 95% LTV products with more flexible income assessment than the high-street banks. Some now consider rental payment history as evidence of affordability. Others will lend up to 5.5x income for higher earners.

This is where broker access matters. Several of these products are intermediary-only, meaning you can't get them by walking into a branch.

## Stage 5: Offer accepted, what happens next

Once your offer is accepted you instruct a conveyancer (a property solicitor) and submit your full mortgage application. Expect:

- **Valuation:** 1-3 weeks after application, often a desktop valuation on standard properties
- **Mortgage offer:** typically 2-4 weeks from full application
- **Searches:** local authority, drainage, environmental. 2-6 weeks depending on the council
- **Enquiries:** your solicitor raises questions on the contract pack from the seller's side
- **Exchange of contracts:** the point at which the deal becomes legally binding and your deposit is at risk if you withdraw
- **Completion:** usually 1-4 weeks after exchange, occasionally same-day

The full process from offer accepted to keys typically takes 12-16 weeks. Chains lengthen this. New-build purchases with long-stop dates can shorten or extend it dramatically.

## Stage 6: SDLT and the first-time buyer relief

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) for first-time buyers in England and Northern Ireland uses a different scale from the standard one:

- 0% on the first £300,000
- 5% on £300,001 to £500,000
- No relief at all if the purchase price exceeds £500,000

A first-time buyer paying £450,000 pays 5% on £150,000 = £7,500. A non-first-time buyer paying the same price would pay £11,250. The relief is meaningful and applied automatically by your conveyancer.

## Practical next steps

- Open a LISA today if you don't have one and you're under 40.
- Pull your credit reports this week and fix any errors before you apply.
- Speak to a broker before you start viewing. Knowing your true borrowing ceiling stops you wasting weekends on properties you can't fund.
